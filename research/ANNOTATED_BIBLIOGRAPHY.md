## 1 · Multi-modal encoding & dual-coding theory

**Paivio, A. (1986).** *Mental representations: A dual coding approach.* Oxford University Press.
> The foundational text on dual-coding theory — the proposal that information encoded through two channels (verbal and non-verbal) produces stronger and more retrievable memory traces than information encoded through a single channel. MBA Rock's text + video + song + calculator lesson architecture is a direct application.

**Mayer, R. E. (2009).** *Multimedia learning* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press.
> The most-cited synthesis of empirically supported design principles for multimedia instruction, including the *modality principle* (people learn better from words + pictures than words alone) and the *multimedia principle* (corresponding words and pictures presented simultaneously). MBA Rock satisfies both.

**Mayer, R. E. (Ed.). (2014).** *The Cambridge handbook of multimedia learning* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press.
> The expanded edited volume covering the empirical literature on multimedia design through 2014. Useful for institutional reviewers wanting to verify the breadth of supporting evidence.

## 2 · Music, song, and memory

**Wallace, W. T. (1994).** Memory for music: Effect of melody on recall of text. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 20*(6), 1471–1485.
> The seminal controlled experiment showing that text learned with a melody is recalled more accurately than the same text learned without melody, at both short and long delays. MBA Rock's 49 original songs operationalize this finding directly.

**Calvert, S. L., & Tart, M. (1993).** Song versus prose forms for students' very long-term, long-term, and short-term verbatim recall. *Journal of Applied Developmental Psychology, 14*(2), 245–260.
> Extends Wallace's finding to verbatim recall across short, long, and very long delay conditions. Song-form prose produced stronger retention across all three time horizons in this study.

**Rainey, D. W., & Larsen, J. D. (2002).** The effect of familiar melodies on initial learning and long-term memory for unconnected text. *Music Perception, 20*(2), 173–186.
> Extends the music-memory literature to *familiar* melodies — showing that melodies learners already know can serve as memory scaffolds for newly encoded text.

## 3 · Spaced practice (the spacing effect)

**Ebbinghaus, H. (1913).** *Memory: A contribution to experimental psychology* (H. A. Ruger & C. E. Bussenius, Trans.). Teachers College, Columbia University. (Original work published 1885)
> The original empirical documentation of the spacing effect — over 130 years ago. Still cited as the foundational reference.

**Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006).** Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. *Psychological Bulletin, 132*(3), 354–380.
> The meta-analysis covering over 800 quantitative comparisons. The single most authoritative source on the magnitude of the spacing effect in the verbal-learning literature.

**Cepeda, N. J., Vul, E., Rohrer, D., Wixted, J. T., & Pashler, H. (2008).** Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention. *Psychological Science, 19*(11), 1095–1102.
> Maps the optimal inter-study interval as a function of the retention delay being targeted. Foundational for curricular pacing decisions.

## 4 · Retrieval practice (the testing effect)

**Roediger, H. L., III, & Karpicke, J. D. (2006).** Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. *Psychological Science, 17*(3), 249–255.
> The controlled experiment that re-established retrieval practice as the most-replicated finding in modern learning sciences. MBA Rock's per-lesson quizzes operationalize this directly.

**Karpicke, J. D., & Roediger, H. L. (2008).** The critical importance of retrieval for learning. *Science, 319*(5865), 966–968.
> Follow-up paper in *Science* demonstrating durable retention gains from retrieval practice across a wide range of materials and learner populations.

**Bjork, E. L., & Bjork, R. A. (2011).** Making things hard on yourself, but in a good way: Creating desirable difficulties to enhance learning. In M. A. Gernsbacher, R. W. Pew, L. M. Hough, & J. R. Pomerantz (Eds.), *Psychology and the real world: Essays illustrating fundamental contributions to society* (pp. 56–64). Worth Publishers.
> Synthesizes the *desirable difficulties* concept — that techniques like spacing, retrieval, and interleaving feel harder during learning but produce stronger long-term retention.

## 5 · Authentic assessment & portfolio-based evaluation

**Wiggins, G. (1990).** The case for authentic assessment. *Practical Assessment, Research & Evaluation, 2*(2).
> The foundational essay introducing the term *authentic assessment*. The MBA Rock Capstone Dossier is a direct application of Wiggins's argument to business education.

**Newmann, F. M., & Wehlage, G. G. (1995).** *Successful school restructuring: A report to the public and educators.* Center on Organization and Restructuring of Schools, University of Wisconsin-Madison.
> Extends Wiggins's idea into the concept of *authentic intellectual work* — tasks with utility beyond the institutional context. The Operator's Dossier is exactly this kind of task.

**Darling-Hammond, L., & Snyder, J. (2000).** Authentic assessment of teaching in context. *Teaching and Teacher Education, 16*(5–6), 523–545.
> Applies the authentic-assessment framework to professional education. Useful precedent for adopting the framework in graduate business contexts.

## 6 · Self-regulated adult learning (andragogy)

**Knowles, M. S. (1980).** *The modern practice of adult education: From pedagogy to andragogy* (Revised and updated). Cambridge.
> The foundational text on andragogy — the practice of teaching adults. Establishes the four characteristics of adult learners that MBA Rock's self-paced architecture directly addresses.

**Zimmerman, B. J. (2002).** Becoming a self-regulated learner: An overview. *Theory Into Practice, 41*(2), 64–70.
> The accessible-summary article on the self-regulated learning framework. Describes the metacognitive, motivational, and behavioral processes that self-paced curricula must support.

**Pintrich, P. R. (2000).** The role of goal orientation in self-regulated learning. In M. Boekaerts, P. R. Pintrich, & M. Zeidner (Eds.), *Handbook of self-regulation* (pp. 451–502). Academic Press.
> Extends Zimmerman's framework with the role of learner goal orientation in self-regulated study.

## 7 · Active & project-based learning

**Freeman, S., Eddy, S. L., McDonough, M., Smith, M. K., Okoroafor, N., Jordt, H., & Wenderoth, M. P. (2014).** Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 111*(23), 8410–8415.
> The PNAS meta-analysis demonstrating active learning's performance advantage over passive lecture across STEM contexts. The general design principle — active over passive — extends to business education.

**Ericsson, K. A., Krampe, R. T., & Tesch-Römer, C. (1993).** The role of deliberate practice in the acquisition of expert performance. *Psychological Review, 100*(3), 363–406.
> The foundational paper on *deliberate practice*. Establishes that the structure of practice — not just the volume — determines expertise development. MBA Rock's "take action" prompts operationalize deliberate practice in the operator's domain.

## 8 · Universal Design for Learning (UDL)

**Rose, D. H., & Meyer, A. (2002).** *Teaching every student in the digital age: Universal design for learning.* ASCD.
> The book that introduced the UDL framework into mainstream educational practice. MBA Rock's design — multiple means of representation, action, and engagement — is built against this framework.

**CAST. (2018).** *Universal Design for Learning Guidelines version 2.2.* Wakefield, MA: CAST.
> The current version of the operational UDL guidelines. Useful for institutional partners assessing UDL compliance in adopted curricula.

---

## 9 · Industry context & enrollment trends

**Graduate Management Admission Council (GMAC).** *Application Trends Survey Report* (annual). Reston, VA: GMAC.
> The authoritative annual report on graduate business school application trends in the U.S. and globally. Useful for grounding enrollment-trend claims in institutional sales conversations.

**Allen, I. E., & Seaman, J. (2017).** *Digital Learning Compass: Distance Education Enrollment Report 2017.* Babson Survey Research Group.
> The most-cited series on distance-education enrollment trends in U.S. higher education. Subsequent annual reports update the picture.
