Cash Flow vs. Profit: The Single Most Expensive Lesson in Business

A profitable company can run out of cash. A cash-rich company can be unprofitable. Both happen all the time, and most founders learn this the hard way — usually at the moment they can't make payroll despite a P&L that says they're up 14% year over year.

The single line that fixes this confusion: profit is opinion, cash is fact.

The gap in one sentence

Net income is what your accountant says happened after assumptions about depreciation, accruals, and revenue recognition. Cash is the money in the bank — and only the money in the bank. The gap between them is called working capital: money tied up in receivables, inventory, and prepaid expenses.

When you grow, working capital usually grows faster than revenue. That growth sucks cash out of the business even when the P&L looks great. It's why fast-growing companies miss payroll. It's why "we're profitable" doesn't mean "we have money."

Two rules that flow from this

Track cash weekly, not monthly. The variance between a P&L month-end and a real bank balance can swallow a quarter. If you only look at numbers once a month, you'll discover the gap when it's too big to fix without raising or firing.

Negative working capital is a superpower. When customers pay you before you pay suppliers (SaaS prepayment, gym memberships, Amazon's float), growth funds itself instead of starving it. Build for negative working capital wherever you can.

The math operators actually use

Two formulas. Memorize both.

Operating cash flow = Net income + non-cash expenses (depreciation, amortization) − Δ Working capital
Runway (months) = Cash on hand ÷ Monthly burn rate

Your runway is the literal number of months you have left if nothing changes. It is the most important number on your dashboard. Update it every Friday.
