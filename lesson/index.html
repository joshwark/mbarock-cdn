Core concepts

4 ideas

'For [target] who [problem], we are [category] that [unique benefit], unlike [alternative], because [proof].' Every word is a choice with explicit non-customers.

Volvo = safety. Disney = magic. Apple = simple. Strong brands compress to one word. Your job is to choose the word, then defend it for a decade.

Position (one sentence) → pillars (3-5) → proof points (3 per pillar) → channel adaptations (copy variants). Every piece of content traces upward to the position.

(1) Trying to be all things — diluted word. (2) Drifting position quarter to quarter. (3) Copying competitors' words instead of owning your own.

Deeper dive

Long-form

Positioning is the most-leveraged decision in marketing. Get it right and copy writes itself. Get it wrong and every campaign fights the brand. The companies that compound brand equity over decades — Patagonia, Costco, Apple, In-N-Out — share one trait: a positioning thesis they refuse to soften, even when growth could come faster by widening the aperture.

The failure mode is always the same. A founder writes a sharp positioning line in year one. By year three, sales asks for a softer version. By year five, marketing has rewritten it to address three customer types instead of one. By year seven, no one inside the company can recite it, and no one outside the company can remember the brand.

The discipline isn't drafting the positioning. The discipline is defending it.
